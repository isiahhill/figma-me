Built to be read alone

01The recommendation

Build email production first

Email production is the one I would build first. This page is the argument and the running proof.

86tests passing
12 of 12scenarios passing
11steps in the pipeline
4steps the model touches

The email team gets 40 to 60 requests a month. Each takes about two hours to build and two weeks to reach the front of the queue. That gap is the opportunity.

What I built takes a request in whatever shape it arrives, asks for anything missing, writes a draft that cites its sources, checks it against brand rules in code, and hands the result to the email team. Nothing sends by itself.

Writing the email is the easy part. Any model does that. The work is everything around it, the part that decides whether a draft may exist.

Why this one and not the other three.↓

These scores are my reading, not a measurement. Anyone can reweight the arithmetic.

Email production

The two-week wait is queue time, not work. It runs every week rather than in bursts, so there is a real baseline to measure against today.

Account-based pages

The strongest revenue story, and the one I wanted to pick. But the lift figure comes from pilots of unstated size, and 25 accounts to 250 is future capacity, not savings today.

Localisation

The widest reach, ten regions. Four asset types across all ten with cultural review is more than a first build should carry, and most of the cost is vendor invoices.

Persona content

Real value, but the revenue effect is hard to isolate from everything else.

The tradeoff, and what would change my mind

Email trades the cleanest revenue story for the only one with a real baseline and daily use. Weight revenue higher and account pages go first, built on the same pieces.

03What it costs today

Two hours of work, two weeks of waiting

A request travels through six stages. One of them is the building.

  1. 01

    A request arrives in Slack or by email

  2. 02

    Back and forth to work out what was meant

  3. 03

    Copy, design, and revisions

  4. 04

    Two weeks in the queue

  5. 05

    The email team builds it, about two hours

  6. 06

    Final check, then send

That is 80 to 120 hours of building a month, before revisions.

The hours are not the interesting part. The queue caps how many campaigns and tests ship, so the experiments worth running never run.

What I assumed, and why

An email team of three to five people. About 50 requests a month. Brand guidelines that split into rules a computer can check and judgment that needs a person. That split is what lets the system stop safely. And stakeholders who preview by link and never learn a new tool.

04How the work flows

Eleven steps, and the model touches four

The model reads the request, writes the draft, rewrites what failed a check, and scores quality. Everything else is ordinary code.

  1. 01

    Read the request and work out what was meant

    AI
  2. 02

    Check the required fields are there, and ask if they are not

    code
  3. 03

    Choose the template and the slots to fill

    code
  4. 04

    Pull in the brand voice, the approved claims, and past examples

    code
  5. 05

    Write the draft, with every fact pointing at its source

    AI
  6. 06

    Check the shape and the brand rules, and stop if either fails

    code
  7. 07

    Rewrite only the parts that failed, once

    AI
  8. 08

    Score the draft, which can send it for a read but never wave it through

    AI
  9. 09

    Decide whether to stop, ask a person to read it, or go ahead

    code
  10. 10

    The requester previews it and approves or asks for changes

    human
  11. 11

    Hand a draft to the sending tool, where a person does the final check

    system

the model decidesordinary codea personanother system

Step nine is the one to look at. It decides whether a draft moves forward, and it calls no model. It is an if-statement reading a rules file.

Asking a model to check its own work gets you the same model twice. A convincing draft talks its way past a model reviewer. It cannot talk past an if-statement.

Step eight is the other half. The quality score can push a draft down to a human read. It can never push one up.

The three ways a person gets involved

It asks a question

Something required is missing, or the system is not sure it understood. It comes back with specific questions instead of a guess.

It asks for a read

Nothing hard broke, but a soft rule fired or the score was low. A person reads it before the requester does.

It stops

A hard brand rule failed. Nothing the model produces overrides that, and no draft leaves.

Put plainly

You paste a request. The system asks only for what it needs, writes a draft that cites every fact, and waits for a person.

What stops a bad email.↓

05What stops a bad email

Three things hold this up

None of them rely on the model behaving well.

The model cannot make web requests. The handoff is a plain function limited to an approved list of create-only endpoints. Nothing here sends, schedules, or triggers.

Subject length, the one-button rule, the allowed link destinations, and the banned phrase list are this brand's own policy, and every violation says so. The unsubscribe link, postal address, and sender identity are structural.

The model writes into a form

It fills named fields. Subject, preheader, body blocks, one button, and a list of sources. Output that does not fit the form is a failed run with no draft. Loose prose never becomes an email.

Brand rules live in code

The rules sit in a file with severities, beside a table of approved claims. A number missing from the table becomes a question, never an invention.

The gate fails closed

It stops on a hard rule, asks for a read on a soft one, and reaches ready only when everything passes. No state means sent.

08Built to be reused

One email type, but the shape a second one inherits

I did not build one email. I built the structure the next content type drops into.

email-cortex/
  brand/      the voice, the approved claims, the locked footer
  schemas/    the shape a request and a draft have to fit
  prompts/    what the model is asked, versioned, with a changelog
  skills/     one folder per email type, with its own settings
  cio/        the short list of things the handoff is allowed to call
  evals/      the test requests and the outcomes they have to produce

Five pieces carry over. The request structure, the approved claims, the rule checker, the test runner, and the locked footer. Account pages would be next.

Marketers own the words

Brand voice, approved claims, and per-type settings are plain files a marketer can edit. Adding an approved claim is a sign-off, not an engineering ticket.

Changes are versioned

Prompts and skills carry version numbers. Changed behaviour means a new version, a changelog note, and a passing test run.

Drift is visible

Expected outputs live in the repository, so a behaviour change shows up as a readable diff.

10Getting people to use it

Shipped is not the same as adopted

Four weeks, one email type, and criteria for stopping written down in advance.

  1. 01

    Week one, two requesters and one reviewer, preview only

  2. 02

    Week two, turn on draft creation

  3. 03

    Week four, expand only if people edit less than 40% of drafts

  4. 04

    Days 30, 60 and 90, look back against the baseline

Nobody learns a tool. A requester pastes what they want and gets a preview link. The email team never leaves the tool they already use.

It stops if people edit more than 60% of what comes out, or if any automatic send path appears.

Adopted means runs that finish without me in the conversation. Not logins, not runs started. Two of five requesters using it is the finding, and it leads the review.

Last, what I left out.↓

11What I left out

What I did not build, and what I would ask for

One slice that runs properly, rather than five that half-run.

Left out on purpose

A live connection to the sending tool, Slack intake, automatic revision loops, every other email type, localisation, account pages, and any send path.

What I am least sure about

Which object in the sending tool the handoff should create. I held it as a setting, a one-line change either way. I have not confirmed where the team's sends live.

What I would ask for

Four weeks on one email type, stopping criteria agreed first, and the test suite gating every write.
